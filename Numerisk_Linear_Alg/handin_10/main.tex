\noindent To find the rest of the eigenvalues needed to construct the basis for $A$, the characteristic polynomial for $A$ is used:
\begin{align*}

\begin{align*}
    p(\lambda) &= \det(A-\lambda I_3) = 
    \det\begin{bmatrix}
        0.5 - \lambda & 0.25 & 0.25 \\
        0.25 & 0.3 - \lambda & 0.45 \\
        0.25 & 0.45 & 0.3 - \lambda

    &= (0.5 - \lambda) \left( (0.3 - \lambda)^2 - 0.45^2 \right) - 0.25 \left( 0.25(0.3 - \lambda) - 0.25(0.45) \right) + 0.25 \left( 0.25(0.45) - 0.25(0.3 - \lambda) \right) \\

\end{align*}
That was a lot of math...But it's not done yet, to extract the remaining eigenvalues a polynomial of the second degree is needed, not this wet dirty cloth of a third degree polynomial. BUT with the pwoer of 23.1~\cite{NUMLA}, by knowing $\lambda_0 = 1$ the polynomial can be factored.
\begin{equation*}
    -\lambda^3 + 1.1\lambda^2 - 0.0625\lambda - 0.0375 = (\lambda-1)q(\lambda)
\end{equation*}
where $q(\lambda)$ is polynomial of $n-1$ degrees, which can be found by coefficient comparison.
\begin{equation*}
    -\lambda^3 + 1.1\lambda^2 - 0.0625\lambda - 0.0375 = (\lambda -1)(A\lambda^2 + B\lambda + C) = A\lambda^3 + (B-A)\lambda^2 + (C-B)\lambda - C 
\end{equation*}
It's known that $A = -1$ which is then used to determine $B$ and  $C$.
\begin{align*}
    A &= -1 \\
    B - A &= 1.1 \implies B = 0.1 \\
    C - B &= -0.0625 \implies C = 0.0375 \\
    -C &= -0.0375
\end{align*}
Then inserting the values of $A$, $B$ and $C$ into $q(\lambda)$.
\begin{equation*}
    -\lambda^3 + 1.1\lambda^2 - 0.0625\lambda - 0.0375 = (\lambda - 1)(-\lambda^2 + 0.1\lambda + 0.0375) = 0
\end{equation*}
Now, since $\lambda_0 = 1$, is only possible if $(\lambda - 1) = 0$. this leaves the second term to be solved, which luckily for us is a second degree polynomial.
\begin{equation*}
    -\lambda^2 + 0.1\lambda + 0.0375 = 0
\end{equation*}
Quadratic formula go!
\begin{equation*}
    \lambda = \frac{-0.1 \pm \sqrt{(0.1)^2 - 4(-1)(0.0375)}}{2(-1)} = \frac{-0.1 \pm 0.4}{-2}
\end{equation*}
\begin{align*}
\lambda_1 &= \frac{-0.1 + 0.4}{-2} = -0.15 \\
\lambda_2 &= \frac{-0.1 - 0.4}{-2} = 0.25
\end{align*}
Wich give the eigenvalues of $A$ as $\lambda_0 = 1$, $\lambda_1 = -0.15$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.25$.
